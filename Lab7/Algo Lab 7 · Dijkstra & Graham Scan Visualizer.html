Why Dijkstra fails with negative edges

Dijkstra is greedy: once a node is settled its distance is treated as final, because every other route must pass through nodes that are at least as far. A negative edge breaks this: a longer route can become shorter later, but the node is already closed. Bellman-Ford fixes this by relaxing every edge V−1 times (O(V·E)), and one extra round detects a negative cycle.
